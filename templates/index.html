Calibrated credit-default probabilities and review tiers

Companion to a study of 10 machine-learning models on Lending Club loans (2007–2018). This app serves one pre-trained, calibrated model; it does not train anything.

What the study found

  • Calibration, not the cutoff, carries the decision. After calibration the Youden-optimal threshold lies close to the default prevalence (π), whatever the class weighting (mean |t*−π| .004–.006 on the three Lending Club sets, .014 on Taiwan, .044 on the 1,000-row German Credit set).
  • No cutoff beats approving everyone on mature vintages at 0–3% discount (in-time, H5), and the expected-profit rule did not beat it out of time (H6); the exception is an 8% hurdle rate.
  • Boosted trees are interchangeable: LightGBM, XGBoost, CatBoost and gradient boosting are statistically equivalent (±.005 AUC); combining tree ensembles (soft voting, stacking) adds at most +0.0007 AUC (H17); tuning gives no meaningful gain for the boosted models (at most +0.0023), and only random forest gains materially from tuning (+0.0224, H16).
  • Calibration drifts out of time, so the calibrator must be refit on recent labelled loans (at least annually).

What this app outputs

  • A calibrated probability of default (PD) from tuned LightGBM.
  • One of three review tiers anchored on π = {{ "%.3f"|format(pi) }}: PD < 0.75π, 0.75π–1.5π, > 1.5π. These are review tiers, not accept/reject cutoffs.
  • The top-3 reason codes from an interpretable EBM (exact additive contributions).

Limits. Trained on funded loans issued {{ meta.training_issue_date_min }} to {{ meta.training_issue_date_max }} (mature vintages). Not validated for lending, fairness or regulatory use; whether reason codes satisfy adverse-action-notice rules is a legal question outside the study.
